OpenID Connect Claims Aggregation 1.0 - Draft 04

Abstract

An OpenID Provider (OP) can disclose claims about an End-User, such as a name or date of birth, to a Relying Party (RP) with the End-User's consent. This document profiles the Aggregated Claims model defined in Section 5.6.2 of OpenID Connect Core 1.0 OpenID.Core. In this model, the OP returns claims asserted by one or more Claims Providers (CPs).¶

This document specifies the following interactions.¶

  • The OP discovers CP metadata.¶
  • The OP establishes itself as a client of the CP.¶
  • The OP obtains claims from the CP.¶
  • The RP requests claims from the OP.¶
  • The OP returns claims from the CP to the RP as Aggregated Claims.¶
  • The RP verifies the returned claims.¶

Introduction

OpenID Connect is a selective claims disclosure mechanism. When a set of claims included in its response is about an entity authentication event, it works as an entity authentication federation mechanism, but it can deliver other claims selected by the subject as well.¶

An OpenID Connect response can include claims from several sources. These sources can be authoritative or corroborative. The roles in the Aggregated Claims model are described in Section 5.¶

An OP can return claims from CPs to an RP by value or by reference. With Aggregated Claims, the OP obtains the claims from the CPs and returns them by value. With Distributed Claims, the OP returns references and access information so that the RP can obtain the claim values.¶

OpenID Connect Core defines the representation of Aggregated Claims returned by an OpenID Provider to a Relying Party, but does not prescribe the OP-to-CP protocol for obtaining the JWT used in that representation. In particular, it does not require the JWT to be a UserInfo response. This specification provides implementation guidance for that interaction and defines an OAuth-protected Claims Endpoint, together with discovery, authorization, account binding, storage, disclosure, and verification requirements. Its scope is Aggregated Claims; the endpoint used by an RP to retrieve Distributed Claims in Section 5.6.2 of OpenID.Core is distinct from the OP-to-CP collection interface defined here.¶

The Claims Endpoint separates OP-facing account-binding information from the CP-signed Claim Set disclosed to the RP. This permits a Claim Set without a sub claim: the OP verifies the CP account association when collecting it and preserves that association when storing and disclosing it. As described in Section 6, the RP relies on the OP for Subject binding. Omitting sub does not prevent correlation through reuse of the same JWT or through identifying attributes. The Subject can authorize reuse of stored Claim Sets or request new issuance. Privacy Considerations describes the tradeoffs for privacy and availability.¶

During Setup, the Subject authorizes the OP to collect claims from a specific CP. The Subject can authorize collection before choosing an RP.¶

The Subject separately authorizes the OP to disclose claims to each RP. Section 10.4 describes how the OP obtains and reuses RP-specific authorization. When prior CP authorizations still cover collection, the OP can avoid new CP consent screens during an RP request.¶

The OP obtains an access token and, if needed and granted, a refresh token. The CP's Claims Endpoint returns signed Claim Sets separately from OP-facing account-binding information. Profiles and trust frameworks may require the use of refresh tokens.¶

The specification has four phases.¶

  1. Discovery Phase. The OP discovers CP metadata.¶
  2. Client Configuration Phase. The OP establishes itself as a client of the CP.¶
  3. Setup Phase. The Subject authorizes the OP to obtain claims from the CP. The OP obtains an access token and may obtain a refresh token.¶
  4. Delivery Phase (RP Phase). The RP sends an authentication and claims request to the OP. The OP obtains any needed Claim Sets from the CPs and returns Aggregated Claims. The RP verifies the response.¶

This document is intended for developers and systems architects who build systems that use attributes and claims.¶

5.2. Subject (User)

The Subject authorizes a CP to give the OP access to claims. The Subject directs the OP to collect those claims and can authorize storage for later use.¶

The OP can combine claims from several sources for disclosure under the Subject's direction. The Subject can authorize collection before choosing which RPs will receive the claims. The Subject can let an RP request claims from the OP to obtain a service. Section 10.4 describes how the Subject authorizes disclosure to an RP.¶

5.3. RP

An RP typically provides a service to the Subject. It requests claims about the Subject from the OP to provide that service. The RP can rely on contract performance, consent, or another lawful basis to process the claims.¶

5.4. CP

The Claims Provider (CP) is an OpenID Provider that accepts the aggregating OP as a client. The CP obtains the Subject's authorization for collection during Setup. It issues signed Claim Sets through its Claims Endpoint. The CP also supplies the account reference that the OP uses to associate each set with the Subject.¶

5.5. OP

The aggregating OpenID Provider (OP) acts as an OpenID Connect client of the CP. It serves RPs as their OpenID Provider. At the Subject's direction, it collects Claim Sets and can store them for later use. It associates each Claim Set with the Subject's OP account. It returns the CP's signed Claim Sets as Aggregated Claims to RPs that the Subject authorizes.¶

6. Trust Model

The RP relies on the OP to link each CP-signed Claim Set to the authenticated Subject. The RP validates the enclosing ID Token or UserInfo response under OpenID.Core. The RP verifies the CP-signed Claim Set separately under Section 10.7. The OP asserts that each included Claim Set concerns the Subject in the enclosing response's sub claim.¶

  1. The OP MUST obtain the CP's iss and sub from the validated Setup ID Token.¶
  2. The OP MUST bind those identifiers to the authenticated OP account that initiated Setup.¶
  3. The OP MUST bind the corresponding CP grant to the same OP account.¶
  4. The OP MUST bind the grant's access and refresh tokens to the same OP account.¶
  5. The OP MUST preserve that binding when refreshing tokens.¶
  6. The OP MUST preserve that binding when downscoping tokens.¶
  7. The OP MUST use CP tokens only for their bound OP account.¶
  8. The OP MUST use Claim Sets it obtains with those tokens only for that account.¶

The OP checks the binding at collection as specified in Section 10.5.6. The binding also applies to stored Claim Sets.¶

The OP and CP establish trust for client registration through OpenID.Federation. The CP grant authorizes collection. The Subject authorizes disclosure to each RP under Section 10.4.¶

  1. The RP MUST apply its trust policy to decide whether to trust the OP's Subject binding.¶
  2. The RP MUST apply its trust policy to decide whether to trust each CP for the claims it uses.¶
  3. The RP MAY use OpenID.Federation to establish trust in either party.¶

A deployment can use Federation Trust Marks to indicate conformance with its trust framework. The CP-signed JWT contains no CP Subject Identifier. The RP relies on the enclosing response and the OP's protected binding to establish which Subject the claims concern.¶

  1. The OP MUST NOT establish an account binding solely from a JWT it imports from another source.¶
  2. The OP MUST restore the protected binding record when it restores a stored Claim Set.¶
  3. The OP MUST validate the protected binding record when it restores a stored Claim Set.¶

An exported Claim Set retains its OP audience. Profiles can define additional Subject binding or presentation-time authentication requirements.¶

7. Discovery Phase

7.1. CP Discovery and Metadata

The OP obtains the CP's endpoints before it sends the Setup authorization request. OpenID.Discovery and OpenID.Federation govern issuer validation for their metadata mechanisms.¶

  1. The OP MUST use validated metadata for the selected CP Issuer Identifier in each Setup transaction.¶
  2. The OP MUST require an equivalent validated issuer binding from any other metadata source.¶
  3. The OP MUST obtain the Authorization, Token, and Claims Endpoints only from metadata bound to the validated issuer.¶
  4. The OP MUST keep each resolved endpoint bound to the selected CP issuer throughout Setup.¶
  5. The OP MAY reuse cached metadata for that issuer while the applicable validity conditions hold.¶
  6. The OP MUST obtain fresh, validated metadata before continuing Setup once those conditions no longer hold.¶
  7. The OP MUST successfully reevaluate CP trust before continuing Setup if the applicable trust rules require it.¶

The metadata mechanism governs cache validity. OpenID.Federation also governs Trust Chain validity.¶

The following parameters apply to the CP's openid_provider metadata.¶

  1. The CP MUST publish the HTTPS Claims Endpoint URL in claims_endpoint.¶
  2. The CP MUST omit URL fragments from claims_endpoint.¶
  3. The CP MUST list its Claim Set signing algorithms in a non-empty JSON array named claims_signing_alg_values_supported.¶
  4. The CP MUST advertise only asymmetric JWS signing algorithms in that array.¶
  5. The CP MUST include RS256 in that array.¶
  6. The CP MUST support each advertised algorithm when the OP selects it in a Claims Endpoint request.¶
  7. The CP MUST set authorization_response_iss_parameter_supported to true.¶
  8. The CP MUST include code in response_types_supported.¶
  9. The CP MUST include S256 in code_challenge_methods_supported.¶
  10. The CP MUST include the same values for these parameters in any OpenID.Discovery configuration document it publishes.¶

RFC9207 defines the authorization response issuer capability. Section 9 specifies Authorization Code Flow and PKCE for Setup. The CP advertises Claim Set signing algorithms separately from UserInfo algorithms.¶

  1. The OP MUST keep the Claims Endpoint bound to the validated CP issuer.¶
  2. The OP MUST keep the advertised Claim Set signing algorithms bound to that issuer.¶
  3. The OP MUST abort collection if its local policy permits none of the CP's advertised Claim Set signing algorithms.¶

7.1.1. Claims Endpoint

  1. The CP MUST expose a Claims Endpoint.¶

The Claims Endpoint is an OAuth 2.0 protected resource where the OP obtains CP-signed Claim Sets. The Claims Endpoint differs from the UserInfo and Distributed Claims endpoints in OpenID.Core. See Section 10.5.5 and Section 10.5.6 for the wire protocol.¶

7.2. Claim Set Profile

This profile applies to Claim Sets from the Claims Endpoint. It uses the JWT typ in Section 10.5.6.1. The profile defines no new metadata or Authentication Request parameter for negotiating the type. Section 10.7 specifies the validation rules. RFC7518 defines the signing algorithm RS256.¶

  1. Deployments MAY establish OP and RP profile support through configuration, registration arrangements, or a trust framework.¶
  2. The CP MUST support the format in Section 10.5.6.1.¶
  3. The CP MUST implement RS256 signing for Claim Sets.¶
  4. The OP MUST implement RS256 signature verification for Claim Sets.¶
  5. The RP MUST implement RS256 signature verification for Claim Sets.¶
  6. The OP MUST support selecting RS256 in Claims Endpoint requests.¶
  7. Implementations MAY support other asymmetric algorithms subject to Section 10.7.3.¶

The following rules apply if Claim Set validation fails.¶

  1. The OP MUST NOT silently fall back to an ID Token, UserInfo JWT, or another profile.¶
  2. The RP MUST NOT silently fall back to an ID Token, UserInfo JWT, or another profile.¶

Stored JWTs with sub require a separate profile and deployment arrangement.¶

7.3. Aggregating OP Metadata

The following parameters apply to the aggregating OP's openid_provider metadata.¶

  1. The OP MUST include aggregated in claim_types_supported.¶
  2. The OP MUST set claims_parameter_supported to true.¶
  3. The OP MUST include S256 in code_challenge_methods_supported.¶
  4. The OP MUST advertise these capabilities in any OpenID.Discovery configuration document it publishes.¶
  5. The OP MUST support Aggregated Claims as an OpenID Provider under OpenID.Core.¶

Section 10.3 governs PKCE for RPs. A CP that only issues Claim Sets to OPs has no obligation to advertise aggregated.¶

8. Client Configuration Phase

8.1. Automatic Registration

The OP acts as an OpenID Connect client of the CP. Automatic Registration includes Trust Chain validation and metadata resolution.¶

  1. The OP MUST use Automatic Registration with the CP as specified in OpenID.Federation.¶
  2. The CP MUST use Automatic Registration for the OP as specified in OpenID.Federation.¶
  3. The OP MUST publish openid_provider metadata in its Entity Configuration.¶
  4. The OP MUST publish openid_relying_party metadata in the same Entity Configuration.¶
  5. The OP MUST use its Entity Identifier as both its Issuer Identifier and its client_id at the CP.¶

For example, an OP with the Issuer Identifier https://op.example uses https://op.example as its client_id at the CP and publishes its Entity Configuration at https://op.example/.well-known/openid-federation.¶

8.2. Client Metadata

  1. The OP MUST include policy_uri in its openid_relying_party metadata.¶

OpenID.Registration defines policy_uri. The CP uses it in Section 9.2. Section 10.5.5 governs Claim Set signing algorithm selection. UserInfo signing and encryption metadata apply to UserInfo responses.¶

8.3. OP Client Authentication

  1. The OP MUST act as a confidential client of the CP.¶
  2. The OP MUST support private_key_jwt client authentication.¶
  3. The CP MUST support private_key_jwt client authentication.¶

The OP declares one token_endpoint_auth_method in its openid_relying_party metadata. The declaration applies to every CP that uses that Entity Configuration. OpenID.Core defines private_key_jwt. OpenID.Federation governs client authentication during Automatic Registration, including PAR.¶

  1. The OP MUST declare private_key_jwt in the base profile.¶
  2. The OP MUST authenticate with its declared method at the CP's Token Endpoint.¶
  3. A profile or trust framework MAY require self_signed_tls_client_auth for all participating OPs and CPs.¶
  4. The OP MUST declare self_signed_tls_client_auth when its profile or trust framework requires it.¶
  5. The OP MUST NOT use none or shared-secret client authentication.¶

mTLS support is optional in the base profile.¶

The CP advertises these methods in token_endpoint_auth_methods_supported in its openid_provider metadata. The following requirements also apply to any OpenID.Discovery configuration document the CP publishes.¶

  1. The CP MUST advertise private_key_jwt.¶
  2. The CP MUST advertise self_signed_tls_client_auth if it supports that method.¶
  3. At other authenticated CP endpoints, the OP MUST use a mutually supported method consistent with resolved metadata.¶
  4. The OP MUST send each authenticated request to the endpoint in the selected CP's validated metadata.¶

The following requirements apply to private_key_jwt at each CP endpoint that uses it. Examples include the Token, PAR, and revocation endpoints. Sections 4 and 5 of RFC10070 specify the audience restriction for JWT client authentication at the Token and PAR endpoints.¶

  1. The OP MUST set aud to the validated CP Issuer Identifier as a string or single-element array.¶
  2. The OP MUST obtain that identifier as specified in Section 7.¶
  3. The CP MUST reject an assertion if its aud fails these requirements.¶
  4. The CP MUST validate the assertion signature with the OP's resolved openid_relying_party keys.¶
  5. The OP SHOULD use short-lived assertions.¶
  6. The OP MUST follow any assertion lifetime limits from its profile or trust framework.¶
  7. The CP MUST reject assertion reuse.¶
  8. The CP MUST retain each accepted (client_id, jti) pair through exp, including any permitted clock skew.¶

An assertion lifetime of a few minutes is one option.¶

RFC8705 governs certificate and client binding when the OP uses self_signed_tls_client_auth.¶

  1. The CP MUST match the presented certificate against the OP's resolved RP JWK Set at each such endpoint.¶

9. Setup Phase

9.1. OP Collection Authorization

  1. The OP MUST collect claims as the CP's OpenID Connect client at the Subject's direction.¶
  2. The OP MUST request the collection grant with response_type=code under OpenID.Core.¶
  3. The OP SHOULD use scopes the CP supports to request claims and collection permissions.¶
  4. The OP SHOULD request only the claims and operations necessary for the Subject's authorized collection purposes.¶
  5. The OP MUST obtain a grant that authorizes Claims Endpoint access for the claims it will request.¶

The CP or its applicable profile or trust framework maps claim permissions to scopes. claims.userinfo and claims.id_token retain their OpenID.Core meanings. A UserInfo-only token does not authorize Claims Endpoint access. Section 6 governs the binding of the grant and tokens to the OP account.¶

  1. When requesting a refresh token for offline collection, the OP MUST include offline_access in scope.¶
  2. The OP MUST follow the OpenID.Core consent rules when requesting offline collection.¶
  3. The CP MUST follow the OpenID.Core consent rules when authorizing offline collection.¶

9.2. CP Consent Dialogue

  1. The CP MUST show the Subject a consent dialogue.¶
  2. The CP MUST explain that the OP collects signed Claim Sets for RPs at the Subject's direction.¶
  3. The CP MUST link to the OP's policy_uri from its client metadata.¶
  4. The CP MUST require active interaction from the Subject before granting access.¶
  5. The CP MUST explain that the OP can use or disclose collected Claim Sets until expiration, despite grant revocation.¶
  6. The CP MUST explain how the Subject controls further disclosure, including how to stop it at the OP.¶

Section 10.5.4 describes those controls.¶

9.2.1. CP Grant Management

  1. The CP SHOULD let the authenticated Subject review standing grants to OPs through a user interface.¶
  2. The CP SHOULD let the authenticated Subject revoke standing grants through that interface.¶
  3. The CP SHOULD show the claims and operations each grant permits in that interface.¶

9.3. CP Token Issuance and Use

  1. The CP SHOULD issue short-lived access tokens.¶
  2. The CP SHOULD set access token lifetimes according to claim sensitivity and exposure.¶
  3. The CP SHOULD restrict each access token to its Claims Endpoint.¶
  4. The CP SHOULD restrict each access token to the claims and operations that the Subject authorizes.¶

These restrictions apply regardless of the token format. The CP checks resource and access permissions as specified in Section 10.5.5.¶

  1. The CP SHOULD issue sender-constrained access tokens.¶
  2. The OP SHOULD use sender-constrained access tokens.¶

The OP and CP can use certificate binding RFC8705 or DPoP RFC9449. The Claims Endpoint applies the relevant proof requirements in Section 10.5.5. mTLS client authentication alone does not imply certificate-bound access tokens.¶

  1. The CP MUST follow RFC9700 when it decides whether to issue refresh tokens.¶
  2. The CP MUST apply the refresh token scope and resource binding requirements in RFC9700.¶
  3. The OP MUST preserve the account binding when it replaces a refresh token.¶
  4. The CP SHOULD support token revocation as specified in RFC7009.¶

RFC6749 governs refresh token authentication and replacement. The CP can rotate refresh tokens to detect replay. Token expiration, rotation, and revocation govern further collection. Section 10.5.4 describes their effect on stored Claim Sets.¶

9.4. Authorization Request and Transaction Binding

  1. The OP MUST authenticate the Subject before starting Setup.¶
  2. The OP MUST create an authorization transaction for Setup.¶
  3. The OP MUST bind the transaction to the authenticated OP account.¶
  4. The OP MUST bind the transaction to the initiating user-agent session.¶
  5. The OP MUST record the selected CP's resolved metadata in the transaction.¶
  6. The OP MUST record its client registration at that CP in the transaction.¶
  7. The OP MUST record the request's redirect_uri in the transaction.¶
  8. The OP MUST bind any selected CP connection configuration to the transaction.¶
  9. The OP MUST bind any selected tenant to the transaction.¶

Section 9.6 specifies how the OP binds the selected CP issuer. The CP identifies Claims Aggregation clients by their client_id under Section 8.¶

  1. The OP MUST use RFC7636 PKCE with an explicit S256 method for every Setup request.¶
  2. The OP MUST check the validated CP metadata for S256 support before sending the request.¶
  3. The OP MUST abort Setup if that metadata does not advertise S256 support.¶
  4. The OP MUST bind the PKCE verifier to the transaction.¶
  5. The CP MUST reject authorization requests from Claims Aggregation clients without an S256 challenge.¶
  6. The CP MUST apply the authorization server requirements for PKCE in RFC9700.¶
  7. The OP MUST send an unpredictable, transaction-specific state value.¶
  8. The OP MUST securely bind state to the transaction and initiating user-agent session.¶
  9. The OP MUST limit the lifetime of each transaction.¶
  10. The OP MUST accept each transaction at most once.¶
  11. The OP MUST reject expired transactions.¶
  12. The OP MUST bind any nonce it sends to the transaction.¶

OpenID.Core governs validation of the ID Token's nonce.¶

9.5. Authorization Response Session Validation

The OP applies the response checks after either recovery procedure and before it sends the code.¶

  1. The OP MUST match the returned state to the pending transaction before sending the authorization code.¶
  2. The OP MUST verify the completion session's binding to the initiating OP account before sending the code.¶
  3. The OP MUST abort Setup if state is missing or either check fails.¶
  4. The OP MUST abort Setup for expired or previously used transactions.¶
  5. The OP MUST NOT establish the OP account from a URL-carried state or session identifier alone.¶
  6. The OP MUST NOT establish the user-agent session from a URL-carried state or session identifier alone.¶
  7. The OP MUST use one procedure below if the initiating session is unavailable at the redirection endpoint.¶

One-time handle. The following requirements apply when the OP uses a handle.¶

  1. The OP MUST store the authorization response with its pending transaction.¶
  2. The OP MUST generate an unpredictable, short-lived handle for the stored response and transaction.¶
  3. The OP MUST return the user agent to the initiating origin or application with that handle.¶
  4. The OP MUST require the original session cookie or an equivalent session credential from the initiating application.¶
  5. The OP MUST verify that credential's binding to the initiating OP account and transaction.¶
  6. The OP MUST consume the handle at most once.¶
  7. The OP MUST NOT complete Setup based on the handle alone.¶
  8. The OP MUST set the handle to expire no later than the pending transaction.¶
  9. The OP MUST limit the handle's lifetime to the CP code's remaining lifetime if known.¶
  10. If the CP rejects the code as expired, the OP MUST invalidate the handle.¶
  11. If the CP rejects the code as expired, the OP MUST require a new Setup transaction.¶

Reauthentication. The following requirements apply when the OP uses reauthentication.¶

  1. The OP MUST reauthenticate the Subject before sending the authorization code.¶
  2. The OP MUST match the reauthenticated account to the initiating account before sending the authorization code.¶

The following requirement applies to both procedures.¶

  1. The OP MUST limit further redirection to destinations it controls and attackers cannot override.¶

9.6. Authorization Response Issuer Validation

  1. The CP MUST include the RFC9207 iss parameter in every Setup authorization response.¶
  2. The OP MUST bind the expected issuer from validated CP metadata to the Setup transaction and user-agent session.¶
  3. The OP MUST validate the response iss against the bound issuer per RFC9207 before code exchange.¶
  4. The OP MUST reject any Setup authorization response that omits iss.¶

9.7. Token Request and Account Binding

The following provisions apply to the code exchange.¶

  1. The OP MUST use the client registration bound to the validated transaction.¶
  2. The OP MUST use the redirect_uri bound to the validated transaction.¶
  3. The OP MUST use the PKCE verifier bound to the validated transaction.¶
  4. The OP MUST NOT use the response to switch the CP, connection configuration, tenant, or Token Endpoint.¶

The OP validates the CP's ID Token under OpenID.Core before establishing the binding in Section 6.¶

  1. The OP MUST establish the account binding before storing the resulting tokens or Claim Sets.¶
  2. The OP MUST establish the account binding before using the resulting tokens or Claim Sets.¶

The following provisions apply to an existing OP account binding for the same CP issuer.¶

  1. The OP MUST compare the new CP sub with the stored value.¶
  2. The OP MUST treat a changed CP sub as account relinking.¶
  3. For relinking, the OP MUST obtain explicit confirmation from the authenticated Subject before replacing the binding.¶

The CP identity here is the validated (iss, sub) pair. The OP checks the applicable account namespace. For tenant-scoped accounts, that namespace is the tenant.¶

  1. Before creating a binding, the OP SHOULD check whether another OP account already links to the validated CP identity.¶

If the OP finds such a binding, the following provisions apply.¶

  1. The OP MUST reject the new binding or require additional verification under its account-linking policy.¶
  2. The OP MUST NOT automatically transfer the existing binding.¶
  3. The OP MUST NOT automatically remove the existing binding.¶

When the OP chooses verification, the following restriction applies.¶

  1. The OP MUST complete that verification before making CP tokens or Claim Sets available to the new OP account.¶

Deployments can permit a CP account to link to multiple OP accounts.¶

  1. Such deployments MUST define the conditions for multiple bindings in their account-linking policy.¶

10. Delivery Phase (RP Phase)

10.1. Delivery Overview

The Delivery Phase typically includes the following steps.¶

  1. (Claims Request) The RP sends an OpenID Connect Authentication Request to the OP.¶
  2. (Request Verification) The OP validates the request.¶
  3. (Subject Granting) The OP establishes the Subject's authorization for disclosure to the RP as described in Section 10.4.¶
  4. (Claims Collection) The OP obtains suitable Claim Sets as described in Section 10.5.¶
  5. (Claims Delivery) The OP returns the authorized Aggregated Claims to the RP as described in Section 10.6.¶
  6. (Claims Verification) The RP verifies the response.¶

10.2. Claims Request by RP to OP

  1. The RP MUST request claims in an Authentication Request under OpenID.Core.¶
  2. To request individual claims, the RP MUST use userinfo, id_token, or both in claims.¶
  3. The OP MUST support the claims parameter.¶
  4. The RP MUST include policy_uri in its client metadata.¶
  5. The OP MUST return claims from the selected CPs as Aggregated Claims.¶

The OP selects CPs associated with the Subject's account. OpenID.Core defines claims and scope. Selecting a specific CP requires an extension. OpenID.Registration defines policy_uri. The OP uses it in Section 10.4. Section 10.7 specifies how the RP evaluates the CP and the age of its Claim Set.¶

10.3. RP authentication and the request verification

  1. The OP MUST apply the redirect URI matching rules in RFC9700.¶
  2. The OP MUST apply the authorization server requirements for PKCE in RFC9700.¶
  3. The RP MUST apply the protections against CSRF and authorization code injection in RFC9700.¶

This profile adds the following PKCE requirements.¶

  1. The RP MUST use S256 whenever it uses PKCE.¶
  2. The RP MUST securely bind its transaction-specific PKCE verifier to the initiating client and user agent.¶
  3. The OP MUST reject a public RP's request for an authorization code without an S256 challenge.¶
  4. The OP MUST reject requests with code_challenge unless code_challenge_method is explicitly S256.¶

10.4. Subject Granting

OpenID.Core governs authentication and prompt. The OP validates the request before seeking consent.¶

  1. The OP MUST obtain the Subject's RP-specific authorization before requesting CP authorization on that RP's behalf.¶
  2. The OP MUST obtain the Subject's RP-specific authorization before disclosing any claims to that RP.¶
  3. The OP MUST show a dialogue when it seeks new consent.¶

The dialogue uses the client metadata that the OP registered or resolved for the RP's client_id.¶

  1. The OP MUST identify the requesting RP by its client_id in the dialogue.¶
  2. The OP MUST show the RP's client_name if that metadata includes it.¶
  3. The OP MUST identify each claim's CP source using the validated CP Issuer Identifier.¶
  4. The OP MUST show all claims it will disclose, including any additional claims in an embedded JWT.¶
  5. The OP MUST tell the Subject that it will disclose the CP Issuer Identifier to the RP.¶
  6. The OP MUST tell the Subject that it will disclose identifying attributes from the JWT to the RP.¶
  7. The OP MUST link to the RP's policy_uri from that metadata.¶
  8. The OP MUST obtain the Subject's consent through explicit action.¶
  9. The OP SHOULD prefer RP identity information that the applicable trust framework verifies.¶
  10. The OP MUST NOT present unverified RP identity information as verified.¶
  11. The OP SHOULD select language-tagged display values according to the Subject's language preferences.¶

A verified organization_name or a Trust Mark that attests to the RP's identity can supply display information. Signed metadata or a valid Trust Mark alone does not verify every displayed name. OpenID.Registration governs language tags and the use of untagged values. The OP omits account_ref from Aggregated Claims as specified in Section 10.5.6.¶

  1. The OP MAY remember a consent decision for the authenticated Subject and requesting RP.¶
  2. The OP MAY use remembered consent instead of a dialogue only for the same authenticated Subject and RP client_id.¶
  3. The OP MUST limit remembered consent to the claims that the Subject authorizes.¶
  4. The OP MUST limit remembered consent to authorized CP sources, identified by their validated Issuer Identifiers.¶
  5. The OP MUST NOT use remembered consent after the Subject withdraws it.¶
  6. The OP MUST NOT use the CP grant as consent for RP disclosure.¶
  7. The OP MUST obtain fresh consent for prompt=consent.¶
  8. For prompt=none, the OP MUST return consent_required if it lacks valid consent for the disclosure.¶

Section 10.5.4 governs withdrawal. OpenID.Core governs other authentication or interaction errors.¶

10.5. Claims Collection

10.5.1. Collection and Reuse

  1. The OP MAY precollect Claim Sets when both the CP grant and the Subject's authorization permit it.¶
  2. The OP MAY reuse a stored Claim Set only if it meets all validation, authorization, binding, and policy requirements.¶
  3. The OP SHOULD let the authenticated Subject request updated claims from a selected CP.¶
  4. The OP MAY start a new collection using existing Subject authorization and valid CP tokens.¶
  5. The OP MAY apply privacy policies and rate limits to these updates.¶
  6. The OP MUST preserve the original CP-signed JWT when storing or disclosing a Claim Set.¶
  7. The OP SHOULD use the narrowest available access token scope for the claims it needs to collect.¶

The relevant policies include the OP's collection and storage policies. Access token renewal alone leaves the stored Claim Set unchanged.¶

The downscoping recommendation applies when the OP has a refresh token and a suitable narrower scope. OpenID.Core and RFC6749 govern refresh and downscoping.¶

  1. The OP SHOULD request a downscoped access token when its current token grants more access than collection needs.¶
  2. The OP MUST reestablish trust in the CP before sending requests after the Trust Chain expires.¶

OpenID.Federation governs Trust Chain reevaluation. The following requirement applies when the OP needs a new Claim Set but cannot obtain it through a valid access or refresh token.¶

  1. The OP MUST obtain the necessary authorization from the Subject at the CP or omit the affected claims.¶

See Section 9 for obtaining that authorization.¶

10.5.2. Token and Claim Set Storage

Protected material includes stored CP access tokens, refresh tokens, and signed Claim Sets. It also includes private keys for client authentication and proof of possession.¶

  1. The OP MUST prevent unauthorized disclosure of protected material.¶
  2. The OP MUST prevent unauthorized modification of protected material.¶
  3. The OP MUST prevent unauthorized use of protected material.¶
  4. The OP MUST enforce the account and CP bindings in Section 6 when accessing stored material.¶
  5. The OP MUST apply these protections to persistent stores, backups, and operational access.¶
  6. The OP MUST prevent unauthorized access to protected material through logs or diagnostics.¶
  7. The OP SHOULD encrypt sensitive stored material at rest.¶
  8. The OP SHOULD protect encryption keys separately from their encrypted data.¶
  9. The OP SHOULD use key isolation to limit the impact of a compromised component.¶
  10. The OP SHOULD use access controls to limit the impact of a compromised component.¶
  11. The OP MAY store signed Claim Sets under these controls.¶

Deployments choose the storage architecture, including whether the Subject controls the encryption keys.¶

10.5.3. Subject Control of Stored Claim Sets

  1. The OP MUST provide a Claim Set management user interface to the authenticated Subject.¶
  2. The OP MUST let the Subject inspect stored Claim Sets through that interface.¶
  3. The OP MUST show each stored Claim Set's CP source in that interface.¶
  4. The OP MUST let the Subject delete selected Claim Sets through that interface.¶
  5. The OP MUST let the Subject review collection and RP-specific disclosure authorizations through that interface.¶
  6. The OP MUST let the Subject withdraw collection and RP-specific disclosure authorizations through that interface.¶
  7. The OP MUST distinguish deletion, stopping collection, stopping disclosure, and CP grant revocation in the interface.¶
  8. The OP SHOULD direct the Subject to an available CP grant-management interface.¶

For a deletion request, the following requirements apply.¶

  1. The OP MUST remove the selected Claim Sets from active stores.¶
  2. The OP MUST NOT disclose retained copies of those deleted Claim Sets.¶
  3. The OP MUST explain the scope of deletion, including any backup retention.¶
  4. The OP MUST explain when deletion takes effect.¶
  5. The OP MUST tell the Subject that deletion at the OP cannot erase copies it already disclosed to RPs.¶
  6. The OP MUST explain that deleting Claim Sets alone leaves collection authorization in effect.¶

A later authorized collection can obtain those claims again. Section 10.5.4 explains how the Subject can stop further collection and disclosure.¶

  1. The OP SHOULD NOT automatically recollect deleted claims from the same CP without a new explicit instruction from the Subject.¶
  2. The OP SHOULD offer one action to delete selected Claim Sets and withdraw their corresponding collection authorization.¶

An RP request or remembered consent does not constitute that instruction. Backups remain subject to Section 10.5.2.¶

  1. Before restoring backup data to active use, the OP MUST apply the Subject's recorded deletion decisions.¶
  2. Before restoring backup data to active use, the OP MUST apply the Subject's recorded withdrawal decisions.¶

A disclosure history identifies the RP recipients, the disclosed Claim Sets, their CP sources, and the disclosure times.¶

  1. The OP SHOULD let the authenticated Subject review their disclosure history.¶
  2. The OP MUST protect all retained history, including legally retained records, under Section 10.5.2.¶
  3. The OP SHOULD minimize the information it retains for disclosure history.¶

For example, the OP can identify disclosed Claim Sets without retaining their complete signed JWTs. Applicable legal retention obligations qualify the history deletion and restoration requirements.¶

  1. The OP MUST apply the Subject's deletion controls to disclosure history.¶
  2. The OP MUST apply the Subject's backup-restoration controls to disclosure history.¶
  3. The OP MUST exclude records it retains solely for legal obligations from ordinary viewing and disclosure.¶

  4. The OP MUST restrict access to those records to authorized purposes for meeting those obligations.¶

  5. The OP MAY let the authenticated Subject export stored CP-signed Claim Sets unchanged.¶

  6. The OP SHOULD explain the correlation and onward-transfer risks of exporting Claim Sets.¶

Exported Claim Sets remain transferable signed personal data. See Section 12.2. Their audience, account-binding, and verification requirements continue to apply. This profile sets no retention deadline for stored Claim Sets. The Subject can retain expired Claim Sets as records without treating them as valid. Section 10.7 governs acceptance and disclosure of Claim Sets.¶

10.5.4. Authorization Withdrawal and Grant Revocation

RFC6749 and RFC6750 govern grant and token validity for further collection. RFC7009 governs token revocation when the CP supports it. Expiration or revocation of a CP token or grant does not itself invalidate an issued Claim Set. Section 6 governs the account and CP binding of stored Claim Sets.¶

  1. If further collection is necessary, the OP MUST handle token errors through the applicable recovery or reauthorization procedure.¶
  2. The OP MAY disclose an otherwise valid stored Claim Set while the Subject's disclosure authorization remains in effect.¶
  3. The OP MUST stop collection within the scope of the Subject's withdrawal until the Subject authorizes it again.¶
  4. The OP MUST stop disclosure within the scope of the Subject's withdrawal until the Subject authorizes it again.¶

A withdrawal limited to one RP applies to affected disclosures to that RP. The Subject can retain private copies after withdrawing disclosure authorization. The RP applies its own acceptance and freshness policies. Disclosing an otherwise acceptable stored Claim Set requires no new CP authorization or CP request.¶

The errors invalid_token and invalid_grant can have several causes. Neither error alone establishes the scope of revocation or the validity of a stored Claim Set. A failed collection or refresh attempt alone does not require suspending disclosure of an otherwise acceptable stored Claim Set.¶

10.5.5. Claims Endpoint Request

The Claims Endpoint follows the protected resource rules in RFC6749. RFC6750 governs bearer token presentation and errors. RFC9449 governs DPoP presentation and errors. RFC8705 governs certificate binding.¶

  1. The OP MUST send an HTTPS POST to the CP's validated claims_endpoint.¶
  2. The OP MUST use application/json as the request media type.¶
  3. The OP MUST use an access token authorized for the Claims Endpoint.¶
  4. The OP MUST send a JSON object as the request body.¶
  5. The CP MUST reject unrecognized members at the top level of the request.¶
  6. The CP MUST reject duplicate names at the request's top level or within its claims object.¶

The CP compares decoded member names using exact, case-sensitive equality. An extension can define new request members and how the OP learns that the CP supports them. The requested claim names inside claims follow the attribute omission rules below.¶

The base request carries nonce, claims, and signing_alg.¶

  1. The OP MUST include a non-empty nonce string.¶
  2. The CP MUST reject a request without a non-empty nonce string.¶
  3. The OP MUST generate a fresh nonce for each collection operation using a cryptographically secure random number generator.¶
  4. The OP SHOULD use at least 128 bits of random input for the nonce.¶
  5. The OP MUST NOT reuse the RP's authentication nonce.¶
  6. The OP MUST NOT use the nonce as evidence of Subject identity.¶
  7. The OP MUST limit each collection operation's lifetime.¶

The OP establishes the following bindings before sending the request.¶

  1. The OP MUST bind the nonce to the pending collection operation.¶
  2. The OP MUST bind the nonce to the OP account.¶
  3. The OP MUST bind the nonce to the CP issuer and Subject Identifier from Setup.¶
  4. The OP MUST bind the selected signing_alg to the pending operation.¶

The following requirements apply to the request's claim and algorithm selection.¶

  1. The OP MUST include a non-empty JSON object named claims.¶
  2. The OP MUST use member names in claims to identify requested top-level claims.¶
  3. The OP MUST set each requested claim's value to null in the base profile.¶
  4. The OP MUST NOT request iss, sub, aud, iat, exp, nbf, or jti as attributes.¶
  5. The OP MUST include a single asymmetric JWS algorithm in signing_alg.¶
  6. The OP MUST select signing_alg from the CP's validated claims_signing_alg_values_supported.¶
  7. The OP MUST select a signing_alg that its policy permits.¶
  8. The CP MUST reject an unsupported signing_alg.¶

Claim-specific query syntax requires an extension. The claims member does not expand the grant's permissions.¶

For example (the access token and nonce are illustrative):¶

POST /claims HTTP/1.1
Host: cp.example
Authorization: Bearer ACCESS_TOKEN
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "nonce": "request-specific-random-value",
  "claims": {"birthdate": null},
  "signing_alg": "RS256"
}
¶

The base request selects top-level claims, not nested fields within a compound claim. For example, "claims": {"verified_claims": null} requests that top-level claim; it does not convey a selection of particular attributes, evidence, or timestamps within it. A CP or trust framework can define narrow scopes or output sets for specific uses, but this specification does not define a general nested query syntax for the Claims Endpoint. The OP cannot remove nested fields from an already signed Claim Set while preserving the CP signature. All disclosed contents remain subject to Section 10.4 and Section 10.6; the OP obtains a suitable Claim Set or omits it when those requirements cannot be met.¶

  1. The CP MUST limit returned attributes to those that the OP requests and the grant and Subject authorize.¶
  2. The CP MAY omit requested attributes, including for privacy reasons.¶
  3. The CP MUST NOT treat omission of an attribute as an error.¶
  4. The CP MUST NOT imply a reason for omitting an attribute.¶
  5. The CP MUST NOT reject an otherwise valid request solely for an unknown requested attribute name.¶

For example, omission can reflect attribute absence, missing verification, or a Subject's choice to withhold the attribute. Section 10.5.6 defines the empty result.¶

Token and scope errors follow the applicable rules in RFC6750 and RFC9449.¶

  1. The CP MUST give token and scope errors precedence over JSON request errors.¶

The following requirements apply to malformed or unsupported requests, including invalid request structure and unsupported signing_alg values. An unrecognized top-level member or a duplicate name in either object makes the request malformed.¶

  1. The CP MUST return HTTP 400.¶
  2. The CP MUST use application/json as the error response media type.¶
  3. The CP MUST send a JSON object as the error response body.¶
  4. The CP MUST set error to invalid_request in that object.¶
  5. The CP MAY include a human-readable error_description string in that object.¶

The following privacy requirement applies to all error responses.¶

  1. The CP MUST NOT disclose the account reference, claim values, or sensitive reasons for withholding attributes in errors.¶

The OP repeats collection for each CP as necessary.¶

10.5.6. Claims Endpoint Response

The following requirements apply to a successful response.¶

  1. The CP MUST return HTTP 200.¶
  2. The CP MUST use application/json as the response media type.¶
  3. The CP MUST send Cache-Control: no-store.¶
  4. The CP MUST use a JSON object as the response body.¶
  5. The CP MUST echo the associated request's nonce unchanged.¶

The base response also carries account_ref and claim_set when it supplies attributes.¶

  1. The CP MUST include claim_set when it supplies requested attributes.¶
  2. The CP MUST encode claim_set as a non-empty string containing the JWT defined in Section 10.5.6.1.¶
  3. The CP MUST include a non-empty account_ref string when it includes claim_set.¶
  4. The CP MUST derive account_ref from the account that the access token authorizes.¶
  5. The CP MUST set account_ref to the Setup ID Token's sub for that account and OP.¶
  6. The CP MUST NOT echo a caller-supplied account reference.¶
  7. The CP MUST ensure that all Claim Set attributes concern the account that account_ref identifies.¶
  8. The CP MUST omit claim_set when it supplies no requested attributes.¶
  9. The CP MUST omit account_ref when it omits claim_set.¶
  10. The CP MUST NOT represent an empty result with null, an empty string, or a protocol-only JWT.¶

The following successful response carries the Claim Set decoded below. The account reference and nonce are illustrative, and the compact JWS is abbreviated.¶

{
  "account_ref": "cp-sub-7f39",
  "nonce": "request-specific-random-value",
  "claim_set": "<CP-signed-JWT>"
}
¶

If no requested attributes are supplied, the CP returns HTTP 200 with the same content type and cache directive. In the base response, the only member is the operation nonce:¶

{
  "nonce": "request-specific-random-value"
}
¶

This empty result reports only that this collection supplied no attributes.¶

The decoded protected header and payload of that claim_set are:¶

{"typ":"claims-aggregation+jwt","alg":"RS256"}
¶ 
{
  "iss": "https://cp.example",

¶

The example uses the common mandatory-to-implement algorithm RS256. The valid test vector in ClaimsAggregation.TestVectors provides the complete signed CP JWT and the signed OP presentation shown in Section 10.6, along with the test keys and instructions for verifying both at 2030-01-01T00:00:00Z. The response's cache directive prevents HTTP caching; it does not prohibit Subject-authorized storage under Section 10.5.¶

The OP applies the following checks to a purported successful response.¶

  1. The OP MUST reject a response with a status other than HTTP 200.¶
  2. The OP MUST reject a response without the required media type.¶
  3. The OP MUST reject a response whose body fails the specified JSON requirements.¶
  4. The OP MUST NOT treat a response containing error as a successful or empty result.¶
  5. The OP MUST reject duplicate top-level member names using the comparison in Section 10.5.5.¶
  6. The OP MUST ignore unrecognized top-level response members other than error.¶
  7. The OP MUST NOT forward ignored members to the RP.¶
  8. The OP MUST match the decoded response nonce to the pending operation's nonce using exact, case-sensitive equality.¶
  9. The OP MUST reject responses for expired or completed operations.¶
  10. The OP MUST accept at most one response per operation.¶
  11. The OP MUST NOT rebind a completed operation to a new account or operation.¶

Ignoring a member leaves the other validation requirements in effect. An extension establishes OP support before the CP relies on the OP to process an extension member. The CP does not need a global database of used nonces.¶

The following requirements apply when the response lacks claim_set.¶

  1. The OP MUST reject the response if it contains account_ref.¶
  2. The OP MUST treat a valid response without account_ref or claim_set as an empty result.¶
  3. The OP MUST NOT establish an account binding from an empty result.¶
  4. The OP MUST NOT change an account binding based on an empty result.¶
  5. The OP MUST NOT store an empty result as a Claim Set.¶
  6. The OP MUST NOT disclose an empty result as a Claim Set.¶
  7. The OP MUST NOT infer attribute absence from an empty result.¶
  8. The OP MUST NOT infer grant revocation from an empty result.¶
  9. The OP MUST NOT infer Subject withdrawal from an empty result.¶
  10. The OP MUST NOT invalidate an otherwise acceptable stored Claim Set solely because collection returns an empty result.¶
  11. The OP MUST NOT suspend disclosure of an otherwise acceptable stored Claim Set solely because collection returns an empty result.¶

Claim Set validation and storage checks apply only when the response contains a Claim Set.¶

The OP applies the following checks when the response contains claim_set.¶

  1. The OP MUST match account_ref exactly to the validated Setup sub for the operation's OP account and CP issuer.¶
  2. The OP MUST NOT derive the expected sub from the response or access token.¶
  3. The OP MUST NOT replace the expected sub with a value from the response or access token.¶

The OP validates claim_set as required by Section 10.7.1.¶

  1. The OP MUST match the JWT's iss to the operation's CP issuer.¶
  2. The OP MUST verify that the JWT contains at least one requested attribute.¶
  3. The OP MUST verify that it requested every returned attribute.¶
  4. The OP MUST verify that its authorization covers every returned attribute.¶
  5. The OP MUST process the authenticated response, account reference, and exact JWT as one validation unit.¶
  6. The OP MUST NOT recombine those inputs through an unprotected queue or cache.¶

Section 10.7.3 specifies the check against the selected signing_alg. Section 9.7 specifies account relinking.¶

  1. If any response check fails, the OP MUST reject the entire response.¶
  2. The OP MUST NOT store a Claim Set from a rejected response.¶
  3. The OP MUST NOT disclose a Claim Set from a rejected response.¶

Omitting a claim from _claim_names cannot hide it from an RP receiving the JWT. For these checks, attributes exclude iss, aud, iat, exp, nbf, and jti. Section 10.7 governs those protocol claims.¶

The OP preserves the validated association in a protected acquisition record.¶

  1. The OP MUST retain a protected acquisition record for each successfully collected Claim Set.¶
  2. The OP MUST record the OP account in the acquisition record.¶
  3. The OP MUST record the CP issuer and Subject Identifier in the acquisition record.¶
  4. The OP MUST record the acquisition validation result in the acquisition record.¶
  5. The OP MUST record the acquisition validation time in the acquisition record.¶
  6. The OP MUST protect the integrity of the acquisition record.¶
  7. The OP MUST protect the record's association with the exact JWT.¶

A hash stored alongside unprotected data does not by itself provide that protection. A deployment crossing an untrusted processing or storage boundary needs integrity protection across that boundary. For example, an authenticated storage service can enforce this association, or a MAC or signature can protect a record covering both the JWT and binding fields when the storage itself is not trusted. An additional CP-signed receipt can be defined by a profile where independent evidence of acquisition is needed. The deployment cases and limits of that additional evidence are discussed in Section 11.6.¶

The OP applies the following checks before redisclosing a stored Claim Set.¶

  1. The OP MUST validate the protected acquisition record.¶
  2. The OP MUST verify that the current authorization permits disclosure.¶
  3. The OP MUST verify that the Subject has not deleted the Claim Set.¶
  4. The OP MUST verify that the Subject has not withdrawn authorization for this disclosure.¶

Section 10.7.1 requires validation of the stored Claim Set before disclosure. A successful acquisition does not waive later key, trust, expiration, or freshness checks. Reusing a stored Claim Set does not require a new CP operation nonce or CP contact.¶

  1. The OP MUST omit the acquisition envelope from the Aggregated Claims response.¶
  2. The OP MUST omit account_ref from the Aggregated Claims response.¶
  3. The OP MUST omit the operation nonce from the Aggregated Claims response.¶
10.5.6.1. Signed Claim Set Format
  1. The CP MUST issue each Claim Set as a compact, asymmetrically signed JWS containing a JSON object.¶
  2. The CP MUST set the protected header's typ to claims-aggregation+jwt.¶

RFC7515 permits omission of the media type's application/ prefix in typ. This type identifies the inner Claim Set within the acquisition envelope or the OP's response.¶

  1. The CP MUST include iss, aud, iat, and exp in the payload.¶
  2. The CP MUST set iss to its validated Issuer Identifier.¶
  3. The CP MUST set aud to the OP's Entity Identifier as a string or single-element array.¶
  4. The CP MUST encode iat and exp as NumericDate values defined in RFC7519.¶
  5. The CP MUST set exp to a time later than iat.¶

The OP uses its Entity Identifier as described in Section 8. These timestamps describe JWT issuance and expiration. Attribute verification can occur at a different time.¶

  1. The CP MUST omit sub from the Claim Set.¶
  2. The CP MUST omit account_ref from the Claim Set.¶
  3. The CP MUST omit equivalent CP account identifiers that it uses for account binding.¶

The Subject can authorize disclosure of identifying attributes. Section 12.2 describes their correlation risks.¶

  1. The CP MUST place claims for the RP in top-level JWT members.¶
  2. The CP MUST sign the JWT with the signing_alg it accepts in the Claims Endpoint request.¶

The OP can reference those claims in _claim_names as specified in OpenID.Core. The format uses no holder proof or per-presentation identifier. It does not require a jti claim.¶

The name Claims Endpoint and the metadata names claims_endpoint and claims_signing_alg_values_supported restore terminology from an earlier draft. This profile separates the OP-facing account reference from the signed JWT that the RP receives.¶

See Section 13 for the CP metadata and Claim Set media type registration requests.¶

10.5.7. JSON Member Handling Examples

Some JSON parsers silently keep a single value for a repeated member name. Implementers can use a parser with duplicate-name detection or check decoded names as the parser emits them, before converting the object to a map. Checking the resulting map cannot detect duplicates that the parser already discarded. For the response, these envelope checks apply to the top-level JSON object. The claim_set string is validated separately under Section 10.7.¶

The following fragments illustrate member handling. Each fragment is added to an otherwise valid message unless indicated.¶

Table 1
Message JSON fragment or body Expected handling
Request with an unrecognized top-level member "example_option": true Reject with invalid_request.
Request with an unavailable attribute name "claims": {"example_attribute": null} (replacing the original selection) Process normally and omit the unavailable attribute.
Request with an unsupported query value "claims": {"birthdate": {"essential": true}} (replacing the original selection) Reject with invalid_request.
HTTP 200 response with an extra member "example_hint": "informational" Ignore the extra member and apply all applicable response checks.
HTTP 200 response with a duplicate member {"nonce":"first","nonce":"second"} (entire body) Reject the response.
HTTP 200 response with an error member {"nonce":"request-specific-random-value","error":"invalid_request"} (entire body) Reject the response as a successful result, including as an empty result.

10.6. Claims Delivery

OpenID.Core governs claim destinations, omission rules, and the Aggregated Claims representation.¶

  1. The OP MUST apply the applicable request constraints to each response.¶
  2. The OP MUST omit _claim_names entries for claims it cannot supply in an authorized, valid Claim Set.¶
  3. The OP MUST limit each embedded JWT to claims the Subject authorizes for disclosure to that RP.¶
  4. The OP MUST replace or omit any Claim Set with claims outside the RP's disclosure authorization.¶
  5. The OP MUST preserve mappings for other claims it returns, including Distributed Claims.¶
  6. The OP MUST preserve the sources that those mappings reference.¶
  7. The OP MUST omit each _claim_sources entry that no remaining _claim_names entry references.¶
  8. If _claim_names would be empty, the OP MUST omit both _claim_names and _claim_sources.¶
  9. The OP MUST NOT forward CP error details to explain an omission.¶
  10. The RP MUST enforce its application's required-claim policy before completing the requested operation.¶

The RP receives all claims in the embedded JWT, including claims without a mapping in _claim_names. Section 10.5.4 specifies when the OP can use previously collected Claim Sets. The essential flag alone does not make an omission an authentication error under OpenID.Core.¶

For example, the RP requests birthdate and address in the ID Token:¶

{
  "id_token": {
    "birthdate": null,
    "address": {"essential": true}
  }
}
¶

The CP supplies only birthdate. The OP returns the following presentation. Only birthdate has a _claim_names mapping, and the response contains only the source referenced by that mapping. If the RP requires address for its operation, it does not complete that operation.¶

The decoded OP presentation below embeds the same CP-signed JWT received in claim_set in Section 10.5.6, without changing its compact JWS serialization. The inner JWS is abbreviated here. When the OP returns the Aggregated Claims in an ID Token, the normal ID Token requirements still apply.¶

{
  "iss": "https://op.example",
  "sub": "op-vector-subject",
  "aud": "rp-client",
  "iat": 1893455999,
  "exp": 1893456060,
  "_claim_names": {"birthdate": "cp1"},
  "_claim_sources": {"cp1": {"JWT": "<CP-signed-JWT>"}}
}
¶

The enclosing sub identifies the Subject at the OP. The CP's account_ref and acquisition nonce are not included in the embedded JWT or passed on as acquisition evidence to the RP.¶

  1. The OP SHOULD return Aggregated Claims in a Token Endpoint ID Token or a UserInfo response.¶

This limits the front-channel exposure described in Section 11.3.¶

10.7. Claims Verification

10.7.1. Verification Responsibilities

  1. The OP MUST obtain Claim Sets from the CP's Claims Endpoint.¶
  2. The OP MUST NOT substitute a Setup ID Token, a refreshed ID Token, or a UserInfo JWT.¶
  3. The OP MUST validate each CP-signed Claim Set when collecting it.¶
  4. The OP MUST validate each stored Claim Set before disclosing it.¶
  5. The RP MUST validate each embedded CP-signed JWT before accepting any claim from it.¶

10.7.2. CP Metadata and Verification Keys

The following sources supply CP metadata and its verification keys.¶

  • OpenID.Discovery supplies validated provider metadata and application keys via jwks_uri.¶
  • OpenID.Federation supplies application signing keys in resolved openid_provider metadata.¶
  • Trusted deployment configuration or a trust framework can provision metadata and keys for a CP issuer.¶
  1. The verifier MUST obtain the CP's metadata and verification keys from one of these sources.¶
  2. The verifier MUST select a source according to its trust policy for the CP.¶
  3. The verifier MUST NOT substitute Federation Entity Keys for the CP's application signing keys.¶
  4. The verifier MUST match the JWT's iss exactly to the CP issuer in validated metadata or trusted configuration.¶
  5. The verifier MAY use unverified iss for key resolution only as its issuer acceptance and network policies permit.¶
  6. The verifier MUST associate the CP's metadata and keys with that issuer, including in caches.¶
  7. The verifier MUST apply the applicable validity requirements to metadata and keys.¶
  8. The verifier MUST apply the applicable trust reevaluation requirements to metadata and keys.¶
  9. The verifier MUST NOT rely on key discovery or signature verification alone to trust a CP for claims.¶
  10. The verifier MUST NOT rely on prior validation if the signing key becomes unavailable or untrusted.¶

  11. In that case, the OP MUST obtain a replacement Claim Set from the CP or omit the affected set.¶

  12. The CP MUST apply the key-to-algorithm binding rules in RFC8725 when signing Claim Sets.¶

  13. The CP MUST publish each Claim Set signing key's algorithm in every advertised JWK's alg.¶

  14. The CP SHOULD keep retired public keys available through its advertised key sources until their signed Claim Sets expire.¶

  15. The CP SHOULD include its clock-skew allowance in that key retention period.¶

  16. The CP MAY withdraw compromised keys earlier under its deployment's key revocation policy.¶

10.7.3. Signature Validation

RFC7515 governs signature verification.¶

  1. The verifier MUST require an asymmetrically signed JWS.¶
  2. The verifier MUST determine its allowed algorithms before verifying the signature.¶
  3. The verifier MUST require alg in both local policy and the CP's validated claims_signing_alg_values_supported.¶
  4. At acquisition, the OP MUST match alg to the signing_alg bound to the collection operation.¶
  5. The verifier MUST use a public key bound to the validated CP issuer for signature verification.¶
  6. The verifier MUST honor key type, signing use, and algorithm restrictions when selecting a key.¶
  7. The verifier MUST resolve any kid only within the CP's trusted key set.¶
  8. The verifier MUST NOT trust a key source solely because the JWT header identifies it.¶
  9. The verifier MUST bind each key to one algorithm using JWK alg or trusted issuer-specific configuration.¶
  10. If the JWK includes alg, the verifier MUST match it to the JWT's alg.¶
  11. The verifier MUST reject the Claim Set if it cannot establish the key's algorithm binding.¶

10.7.4. Claim Validation

The verifier checks claims after validating the signature. RFC7519 governs time claim types, expiration, and not-before processing.¶

  1. The verifier MUST require claims-aggregation+jwt in the protected header's typ.¶
  2. The verifier MUST reject a Claim Set whose payload contains sub.¶
  3. The verifier MUST require iss, aud, iat, and exp.¶
  4. The verifier MUST require a non-empty string for iss.¶
  5. The OP MUST validate the acquisition or stored binding under Section 10.5.6.¶
  6. The verifier MUST require exactly one aud value as a string or single-element array.¶
  7. The OP MUST match aud exactly to its client_id at the CP.¶
  8. The RP MUST match aud exactly to the OP issuer it validated through OpenID Connect.¶
  9. The verifier MUST compare issuer and audience identifiers without normalization.¶
  10. The verifier MUST require exp to be later than iat.¶
  11. The verifier MUST reject a JWT with an iat in the future.¶
  12. The verifier MAY allow a small clock skew when comparing times to its clock.¶
  13. The verifier MUST apply any clock-skew allowance consistently to iat, exp, and nbf.¶

The following requirements apply when the RP receives Aggregated Claims through UserInfo.¶

  1. The RP MUST use the validated OP issuer from the transaction that issued the access token.¶
  2. The RP MUST NOT infer a new issuer from the CP JWT or an unsigned UserInfo response.¶
  3. The RP MUST preserve the OP issuer's association with that access token.¶

The RP relies on the OP for Subject binding as described in Section 6. The RP follows the same clause when deciding whether to trust the CP.¶

  1. The RP MUST validate the Aggregated Claims structure according to OpenID.Core.¶
  2. The RP MUST check that each mapped claim appears in its referenced JWT.¶
  3. The RP MUST apply the validation rules for each claim it uses.¶
  4. The OP MUST reject a Claim Set that fails any required check.¶
  5. The OP MUST NOT store an invalid Claim Set.¶
  6. The OP MUST NOT disclose an invalid Claim Set.¶
  7. The RP MUST NOT use claims from an invalid Claim Set.¶
  8. The RP MUST treat claims mapped to an invalid Claim Set as unavailable.¶
  9. The RP MAY reject the entire enclosing response.¶
  10. Profiles MAY require rejection of the entire enclosing response.¶

The verification rules in Section 10.7.1 apply to every Claim Set the RP uses. Section 10.6 specifies when the RP can complete the requested operation.¶

10.7.5. Local Freshness Policy and User-Directed Updates

The RP's acceptance checks include Claim Set validation and its freshness policy.¶

  1. The RP MUST apply its application's freshness requirements before using claims.¶
  2. The RP MUST apply its acceptance checks to Claim Sets it uses after OP selection or a user-directed update.¶
  3. The RP MAY enforce a maximum JWT age based on the CP-signed iat.¶
  4. If a Claim Set is too old, the RP MUST treat its claims as unavailable for that use.¶
  5. The RP SHOULD explain to the Subject when updated information is necessary.¶
  6. The RP MAY direct the Subject to the OP's claim-management interface to request an update.¶

The Subject can retry the OpenID Connect interaction after an update. Retrying authentication alone does not require the OP to recollect claims.¶

IDA.Schema defines the verified_claims structure. Using it does not by itself implement the OpenID4IDA query protocol or external assertion format.¶

Implementations that support OpenID4IDA can use its existing request and validation mechanisms. For example, max_age constrains verification.time or another requested date or timestamp.¶

  1. The RP MUST evaluate assurance timestamps separately from the JWT's iat when it uses OpenID4IDA freshness constraints.¶
  2. The OP MAY use supported constraints to select stored Claim Sets.¶

A new JWT does not show when the CP last verified the claims. Meeting a verification-age requirement can require renewed verification at the CP. Mapping constraints into a Claims Endpoint request requires a separate extension or profile. The base claims object accepts only null values. The OP can support OpenID4IDA without forwarding constraints or using them to select stored Claim Sets under this specification. OpenID4IDA still governs its implementers' query, filtering, and omission behavior.¶

The OpenID4IDA external assertion format uses typ=provided-claims+jwt and requires sub. That format prohibits aud and exp.¶

  1. The OP MUST NOT rewrite an existing signed assertion to convert it to this profile.¶

Interoperability with that external assertion format requires a separate profile.¶

11. Security Considerations

11.1. General Security Considerations

The security considerations of OpenID.Core, OpenID.Federation, and RFC8725 apply.¶

Before verifying the signature, the verifier constrains algorithms and keys under Section 10.7.3 and Section 10.7.2. This prevents a JWT header from selecting an unauthorized algorithm or causing use of a CP public key as a MAC secret.¶

  1. The CP SHOULD consider claim sensitivity, change rate, storage duration, and verification key retention when choosing Claim Set lifetimes.¶

Short lifetimes require more frequent collection, while long lifetimes extend the period during which previously issued attributes can be presented. Expiration limits the lifetime of the signed artifact. It does not guarantee that the underlying attributes remain current or that the CP's grant remains authorized. Key withdrawal can make a stored Claim Set unusable before its exp, as described in Section 10.7.2.¶

The type and claim rules in Section 10.5.6.1 distinguish Claim Sets from ID Tokens and UserInfo JWTs. RFC8725 describes cross-JWT confusion. A separate URL or the presence or absence of nonce or auth_time does not by itself distinguish these JWTs.¶

11.2. Setup Transaction and Session Integrity

The issuer-based countermeasure in OAuth.Security.Update protects connections to multiple CPs. Section 7.1 binds metadata to the selected issuer. Section 9.6 checks that issuer before code exchange. That check also addresses the mix-up risk in RFC9700. Section 9.7 preserves the selected client and any connection or tenant context.¶

PKCE binds the code to the OP's token request under Section 9.4. An attacker can still initiate Setup for the attacker's OP account and induce a victim to authorize access to the victim's CP account. PKCE alone does not detect this cross-user session fixation attack, described in Section 2.3 of OAuth.Security.Update, with countermeasures in Section 2.3.2. Before exchanging the code, the OP verifies the completion session's binding under Section 9.5. A valid state received through a URL is insufficient without this session validation. Comparing a previously stored CP subject identifier detects account changes. Checking for the same CP (iss, sub) on another OP account can also detect an attempted new binding when that CP identity is already known to the OP. Neither check establishes a relationship for a CP identity that has never been linked at the OP. The trust assumptions in Section 6 continue to apply.¶

11.3. RP Request Integrity and Broker-Side Consent

The checks in Section 10.3 apply to signed and unsigned requests.¶

The OP brokers requests between RPs and CPs using the broker-side consent approach in OAuth.Security.Update. The CP grant authorizes collection, and the Subject's authorization at the OP governs RP disclosure. Section 10.4 covers both RP-triggered collection and reuse. The Subject can withdraw authorization under Section 10.5.4 even if the CP grant remains valid. Pre-collection can keep the recipient RP and its activity timing from the CP.¶

An ID Token returned from the Authorization Endpoint can expose embedded CP-signed Claim Sets to browser scripts and front-channel response handling. Depending on the response mode and implementation, those artifacts can persist in URLs, including fragments, browser history, or application and diagnostic logs. Unlike an authorization code, the signed personal data can be copied and presented to other parties, subject to their acceptance checks. The back-channel delivery recommendation in Section 10.6 reduces this exposure; it does not prevent an RP that receives a Claim Set from copying it. This is an additional reason to minimize front-channel exposure alongside the leakage risks discussed in Sections 4.2 and 4.3 of RFC9700.¶

11.4. Audience Binding

The Claim Set audience identifies the OP that obtained it. The RP checks that audience under Section 10.7.4 and applies its CP trust policy.¶

  1. The CP MUST establish the client under Section 8 before issuing a Claim Set.¶
  2. The CP MUST NOT let another client use that client_id, including through a separate registration.¶

11.5. Cross-User Claim Substitution

A compromised or malicious OP can include a Claim Set for one Subject in a response for another Subject. The substituted Claim Set can have a valid CP signature and the expected OP audience. The RP's signature and audience checks therefore cannot detect this substitution. The trust model in Section 6 relies on the OP to preserve the account binding through collection, storage, and disclosure. A Federation Trust Mark can attest to an OP's compliance with operational requirements. It does not prove the account binding for a particular response.¶

11.6. Acquisition Response Integrity

11.6.1. Operation and Response Binding

The collection nonce correlates an authenticated CP response with a pending OP operation; it is not an access credential or an issuance-idempotency key. The CP may process a retried request more than once, but the OP accepts at most one response for its pending operation. A holder of a stolen bearer token can request additional Claim Sets with new nonces, so a CP nonce database would not prevent that use. TLS server authentication and the token protections in Section 9.3 address that threat.¶

  1. The OP MUST validate the CP endpoint's TLS server identity.¶

Deployments can impose issuance rate limits without making every CP maintain a shared nonce-replay database.¶

The OP binds the selected signing algorithm to the operation before sending the request and checks the returned JWT against it. A request or response that traverses an untrusted internal processing boundary needs integrity protection across that boundary as described in Section 10.5.6; token sender-constraining alone does not authenticate arbitrary JSON request or response bodies across such a boundary.¶

11.6.2. Trusted Collection and Protected Handoffs

The trusted collection boundary includes the components the deployment relies on to preserve two inputs:¶

  • The authenticated CP response.¶
  • The operation context that the OP selected independently.¶

Those components preserve both inputs until a trusted component validates the response and protects the resulting binding record. The deployment identifies which components it trusts and which compromises it aims to withstand. Common ownership, an OP-managed label, and the number of TLS connections are insufficient on their own. Access controls, account and tenant separation, and authenticated handoffs help enforce the boundary.¶

The base protections suffice when a trusted OP collector receives the response over authenticated TLS, checks it under Section 10.5.6, and protects the complete association between the account and the JWT. The account check compares account_ref with the CP's sub from the validated Setup ID Token. The CP-signed JWT contains no CP Subject Identifier that could independently confirm the association.¶

A TLS-terminating gateway can be part of the trusted collector if the deployment trusts it to preserve the response and operation context. A deployment that aims to withstand compromise of that gateway places it outside the trusted collection boundary.¶

A trusted collector can validate the response and protect the complete binding record before handing it to a queue, cache, or object store outside the boundary. The trusted consumer then verifies that record. An extra CP signature is unnecessary for that handoff alone.¶

An OP MAC, signature, or suitably bound authenticated encryption can protect the exact JWT together with its OP account, CP identifiers, and acquisition context. Section 10.5.6 requires protection of the association as well as the JWT. On backup restoration, the OP applies the current deletion and withdrawal decisions under Section 10.5.3. An authentic older record can predate those decisions.¶

11.6.3. Additional CP-Origin Evidence

An additional CP signature can help when a TLS terminator outside the trusted collection boundary can alter the response before an honest OP component validates it.¶

For example, a gateway could pair a valid Claim Set for CP account B with an acquisition envelope for account A. B's JWT can have the expected OP audience, requested attributes, and selected signing algorithm. The envelope can carry A's account_ref and pending operation nonce. The checks of the account reference and the JWT's issuer, audience, signature, and lifetime can all pass because the JWT has no CP Subject Identifier. If the gateway kept B's account_ref, the account comparison would fail. A MAC that the OP adds after substitution protects the pairing it received. It cannot establish that the CP supplied that pairing.¶

The trusted validator needs to verify that the CP supplied the association between the account and the exact Claim Set. A CP-signed envelope or receipt can provide that evidence. An authenticated connection from the CP to the trusted validator can also protect it if the connection keeps the plaintext from the intermediary. The need for extra evidence depends on which components the deployment trusts.¶

An independently authorized auditor can also need evidence that the CP associated an account with a particular JWT. The relevant evidence depends on the audit question and policy:¶

  • An OP signature can provide the evidence if the audit policy accepts records that the OP creates.¶
  • The existing Claim Set signature shows which attributes the CP signed.¶
  • A CP-signed receipt can show which account the CP associated with the Claim Set. This is useful when the auditor needs evidence from the CP.¶

This audit use requires a separately authorized purpose for access to stable CP account references and acquisition records. That access also requires minimization and retention controls. Normal RP disclosure follows Section 6.¶

11.6.4. Receipt Scope and Reuse Limits

A separate receipt profile would need to bind the following values to the exact signed Claim Set or to a collision-resistant commitment to it:¶

  • A distinct receipt purpose.¶
  • The CP issuer.¶
  • The recipient OP.¶
  • The CP-derived account reference.¶
  • The acquisition operation nonce.¶

The OP uses one identifier here. Its Entity Identifier, Issuer Identifier, and CP-facing client_id are equal. A commitment would specify its algorithm and byte representation. Using the exact compact JWS avoids reserializing decoded claims. The proof protects the pairing only if it covers both the account reference and the JWT or its commitment.¶

A receipt profile would also need to specify trusted key and algorithm selection, pending-operation checks, and what to do if a required proof is missing or stripped. It would define how to handle empty results. This specification defines no receipt format, parameter, or mandatory additional CP signature.¶

A verified receipt authenticates a CP statement under the accepted signing key and policy. It cannot prove that the CP sent an HTTP response or that the OP received or validated it. It cannot establish the truth of the CP's account association or the Subject's consent to later RP disclosure. An issuance timestamp does not prove delivery or a new identity check.¶

The OP still needs its protected account binding. A receipt cannot restore a lost association with an authenticated OP account. A malicious OP can still violate Section 6. A receipt also cannot prevent misuse of stolen tokens, suppression of responses, or rollback of authorization state.¶

A signature provides integrity and evidence of origin. Anyone with the OP's public key can encrypt to it, so encryption to the OP alone cannot establish CP origin. A deployment that needs both confidentiality and CP-origin integrity across a boundary needs a design that protects the contents and authenticates the CP.¶

The Subject's authorization governs disclosure, including when the OP stores encrypted data or holds a receipt. The Aggregated Claims response excludes acquisition receipts. Storage, audit access, and backup handling of receipts remain sensitive-data processing.¶

If a receipt profile sets an initial acceptance deadline, a late receipt cannot establish a new acquisition binding. When the OP validates acquisition in time and preserves the binding in a protected record, expiry of that deadline alone does not require CP contact or suspension of an otherwise acceptable stored Claim Set.¶

A receipt within its acceptance window does not extend the inner JWT's exp. Current key, signature, audience, trust, freshness, and Subject authorization checks still apply. These rules concern acquisition and storage. They add no presentation-time CP status check and leave the grant-revocation rules in Section 10.5.4 in effect.¶

11.7. CP Credential Exposure and Claim Set Replay

Pre-collection permits an OP to hold a grant that covers future Subject-authorized uses beyond a single RP request. The least-privilege requirements in Section 9 and Section 10.5 limit requested access. Downscoping an access token does not by itself reduce the authority of the original refresh token. The protections in Section 9.3 and Section 10.5.2 address the resulting concentration of credentials and personal data at the OP.¶

The assertion audience restriction in Section 8.3 implements the preferred audience-injection countermeasure in Section 2.1.2.1 of OAuth.Security.Update. The OP's Entity Identifier is its client_id at every CP, so uniqueness of client identifiers across CPs is not an available countermeasure. Using different authentication keys for different CPs can provide additional isolation only when each CP's accepted key set is also restricted accordingly. Publishing several keys in a common RP key set does not by itself establish that isolation.¶

The OP Entity manages the client authentication keys in this profile. An app on a device acts as the Entity's user interface or agent. The CP resolves one client identity and one RP metadata object for the OP's Entity Identifier. This profile does not define separate enrollment or authentication bindings for each device instance.¶

Sharing one authentication private key across devices increases the risk of key extraction. Listing all device keys in the common RP JWK Set can make that set grow without bound. It can reveal how many devices the OP uses and how often devices are added or removed. The CP still resolves the same client identity for those keys.¶

A confidential client can run on a device. Device-generated keys and registration for each instance can support secure client authentication. Combining these with the common Entity Identifier, OP issuer, and RP metadata requires a separate profile.¶

Such a profile could use Explicit Registration or Dynamic Client Registration for each instance. It could also let the OP Entity attest to each instance's key while it keeps the Entity Identifier as client_id. The profile would need to define the binding between each instance, its credentials, client_id, and the OP issuer. It would also need rules for selecting the authentication method. Giving an instance a different client_id while keeping a common OP issuer would change the identity relationship in this base profile. These instance profiles are outside the scope of this specification.¶

Client authentication and sender-constrained CP access tokens protect collection, not the subsequent presentation of a signed Claim Set. A Claim Set's audience identifies the OP, not an individual RP or presentation. An RP or another holder can copy that JWT and send it elsewhere. To accept the Claim Set, the recipient validates the enclosing response and the Claim Set under Section 10.7. The recipient also relies on the account binding described in Section 6. Possession of the embedded JWT alone does not authenticate the Subject. A short Claim Set lifetime limits the exposure period. A holder can still copy or reuse the Claim Set before expiration.¶

Under the trust model in Section 6, the RP relies on the OP's account binding and disclosure controls. These protections neither require per-presentation CP contact nor cause CP grant revocation to invalidate stored Claim Sets. Storage remains under the Subject's control within the limits described in Section 11.8.¶

11.8. Freshness and Revocation Limits

The CP's signed iat establishes the JWT's age for freshness checks.¶

  1. The verifier MUST NOT substitute an OP collection timestamp or enclosing ID Token iat for the CP-signed iat.¶

A CP can issue a new JWT containing attributes verified earlier. Applications needing freshness of identity verification or evidence can use the corresponding signed data and validation rules in OpenID4IDA, as described in Section 10.7.5.¶

The Subject controls a signed Claim Set held by the OP. CP grant or token revocation, including unilateral revocation, affects future collection. The Subject can continue to present previously issued Claim Sets until expiration, subject to this profile's validation and acceptance rules. Section 10.5.4 describes the Subject's control over further disclosure at the OP.¶

A CP can choose a short lifetime when the underlying attributes can change quickly. An application that needs prompt status updates, such as for a revoked driver's license, can use a separate authenticated status mechanism and an acceptance policy for the issued Claim Set. Offline verification checks the signed Claim Set. It cannot establish that the attributes still reflect the current state. A current status check supplements that verification and concerns the issued Claim Set rather than the collection grant.¶

12. Privacy Considerations

12.1. OP Visibility and Reliance on Subject Instructions

The OP processes the contents of Claim Sets from CPs and knows which RPs receive them. This combination can reveal attributes and relationships across services. In a deployment where a third party operates the OP and can access that processing or its records, the Subject places substantial trust in that operator. Encryption at rest protects stored data against some compromises; it does not by itself hide data from the OP while processing it. A distributed-claims deployment does not necessarily hide information from the OP either; visibility depends on which components actually obtain and process the data.¶

At Setup, the Subject authorizes the CP to release claims to the OP for future use under the Subject's direction. The CP relies on the OP to enforce the later RP-specific authorization in Section 10.4. A CP signature authenticates the issued data; it does not prove that the OP obtained authorization for a particular disclosure. Federation trust and policy information can inform a trust decision, but do not by themselves prove that the OP honored the Subject's instructions in an individual transaction.¶

The least-privilege and disclosure requirements in Section 9, Section 10.5, and Section 10.6 limit what the OP obtains and discloses. In particular, the RP receives all claims in an embedded signed JWT, including claims not listed in _claim_names. The OP cannot remove individual claims from that JWT while preserving the CP's signature. A deployment seeking stronger limits on OP visibility or finer-grained disclosure needs an appropriate architecture or extension, rather than relying on storage encryption alone.¶

12.2. Correlation and Transferable Signed Data

The OP uses the CP's stable Subject Identifier for account binding. The RP-facing Claim Set omits that identifier. Pairwise identifiers at the CP would otherwise be scoped to the OP's Sector Identifier rather than to each downstream RP, as specified in Section 8.1 of OpenID.Core. Omitting the CP sub removes that direct downstream identifier; it does not guarantee unlinkability. Authorized identifying attributes can still correlate Subjects, and a CP can recognize information it issued when an RP shares it. The OP's pairwise identifiers in enclosing responses do not hide the inner JWT's contents.¶

When the OP reuses a Claim Set, it sends the same CP-signed JWT. An RP can compare the JWTs it receives in different presentations. RPs that share the JWTs they receive can also compare them across services. A matching JWT, including its signature, lets these recipients correlate the presentations. Different enclosing OP responses leave a reused inner JWT intact. Correlation by JWT comparison can therefore occur even when other identifiers are ephemeral or pairwise.¶

Recipients can also correlate newly issued JWTs through stable identifiers, identifying combinations of claims, or distinctive timestamps.¶

An RP can also pass a CP-signed JWT to a third party that can independently verify the CP's signature and the signed contents. This makes disclosure of signed personal data more consequential than disclosure of an unauthenticated assertion. The signature is evidence of what the CP issued, not proof that the Subject presented it to a particular RP or that the attributes remain current. The base format does not cryptographically prevent onward transfer. The Subject's controls at the OP cannot recover copies already held by RPs.¶

Selective disclosure can reduce the attributes revealed to a recipient, but does not by itself ensure unlinkability. For example, Section 10.1 of SD-JWT explains that recipients can correlate reuse of an issuer-signed credential and that colluding issuers and verifiers can recognize the same credential. SD-JWT is an example for future profiles, not a format introduced by this specification. Such profiles would need compatible subject-binding, audience, consent, and verification rules.¶

12.3. Pre-Collection, Batch Issuance, and On-Demand Issuance

Pre-collection and reuse of stored Claim Sets can reduce the CP's visibility into when the Subject uses an RP. If an RP asks the Subject for fresher claims, the Subject can request an update. The OP can then request a new Claim Set from the CP.¶

The CP can observe when Claims Endpoint requests arrive and how often they arrive. If those requests follow RP activity, the CP can use that pattern to infer when the Subject uses an RP.¶

The following recommendations apply when the OP seeks the Subject's collection or disclosure authorization.¶

  1. The OP SHOULD explain that reusing a signed JWT can link presentations.¶
  2. The OP SHOULD explain that updates can reveal RP activity timing to the CP.¶

The Subject can then choose whether to authorize reuse or request an update, subject to the RP's acceptance policy. Section 10.5.1 describes privacy policies and rate limits for user-directed updates.¶

Reuse avoids a Claims Endpoint call for each presentation. The CP can still observe activity through information shared by RPs or retrieval of metadata, verification keys, or status. Valid metadata and key caches tied to the issuer can reduce some such contact, subject to the requirements in Section 10.7.2.¶

An alternative design can pre-issue a batch of distinct credentials for one-time use, combining advance collection with avoidance of a common reused artifact. This introduces inventory and replenishment requirements. Section 10.1 of SD-JWT describes batch issuance for presentation and verifier-verifier unlinkability, including the need to avoid other common identifiers and timing values. It does not solve issuer-verifier collusion.¶

With this profile, the Subject can instead request a newly issued Claim Set for a use, without a CP sub in the presented JWT. This avoids maintaining a stock of unused credentials, but requires CP availability and continued willingness to issue, and exposes issuance timing to the CP. It does not require revealing the RP's identity to the CP. The correlation risks for newly issued Claim Sets are described in Section 12.2.¶

The stable account-binding identifier remains in the OP-facing acquisition envelope. The OP validates it against the Setup ID Token, then discloses only the inner JWT. The new endpoint avoids changing UserInfo's requirement that its sub match the ID Token sub. Using a refreshed ID Token itself would not provide that separation: Section 12.2 of OpenID.Core requires its issuer, subject, and audience to remain those of the original ID Token.¶

This specification introduces no batch issuance protocol and does not require a newly issued JWT per presentation. Reuse of stored Claim Sets and user-directed updates remain Subject choices, subject to RP acceptance policy. These issuance and reuse tradeoffs are not unique to either an OP-mediated or issuer-holder-verifier architecture.¶

12.4. Subject Control of Retention and Disclosure

The controls in Section 10.5.3 let the Subject distinguish stored data from collection and disclosure permissions. They complement the CP's grant-management interface: revoking a CP grant does not retract issued Claim Sets, while withdrawal at the OP controls further OP disclosure. Neither operation retracts data already received by an RP. A Subject may retain a Claim Set as a personal record even after it expires; expiration restricts valid use rather than imposing deletion. The safeguards in Section 10.5.2 address the resulting storage risks without imposing a protocol retention deadline or a particular archival encryption design.¶

Disclosure history can help the Subject identify RPs that already received their data, including when seeking deletion from those RPs. That history is itself sensitive personal data and remains subject to protection, minimization, and Subject deletion controls in Section 10.5.3. Export can give the Subject an independent copy of an issued artifact, but does not remove the transferability or correlation risks described in Section 12.2.¶
